DEATH OF THE NILE (2022) · EXHIBITED AT FULLDOME UK FESTIVAL
01 · OVERVIEW & ARTISTIC DIRECTION

DESCRIPTION & CONCEPT

Project Description

Death of the Nile (2022) is a completed 180 degree fulldome film and interactive spatial work created as my MRes Digital Arts thesis with i-DAT at the University of Plymouth and exhibited at the Fulldome UK Festival.

Placing the audience directly inside the shifting landscape of the Nile River, the work transforms hydrological climate data, rising heat, and diminishing water levels into a physical, surround visual and acoustic experience.

Artistic Concept

Growing up connected to the Nile shapes how you understand a river. It is not background scenery; it is living memory, agriculture, architecture, and daily survival for millions of people.

The artistic concept was to move climate crisis storytelling out of flat charts and distant news reports into a shared dome cinema where viewers feel the river rising, drying, and fracturing overhead.

02 · PRACTICE LED INQUIRY

RESEARCH FOUNDATION

Developed through formal MRes Digital Arts academic research with i-DAT in Plymouth, combining hydrological environmental data with real time 180 degree spherical dome cinema production.

01 · CLIMATE & RIVER DATA

Nile Basin Water Scarcity

Examined hydrological flow shifts, upstream infrastructure pressure, evaporation rates, and ecological vulnerability across the Nile corridor.

02 · FULLDOME CINEMA GRAMMAR

180° Spherical Framing

Researched how horizon placement, zenith tension, and camera velocity behave inside a hemispherical theatre without causing audience disorientation.

03 · REAL TIME ENGINE PIPELINE

Unreal Engine Domemaster

Built a complete real time Unreal Engine rendering workflow capable of outputting crisp 180 degree equidistant fisheye masters for festival projection.

03 · PROCESS FROM START TO FINISH

COMPLETE PRODUCTION JOURNEY (START TO EXHIBITION)

STEP 01 START · RESEARCH

MRes Thesis Framing & Environmental Data Collection

Established the academic and artistic thesis with i-DAT at the University of Plymouth, gathering Nile Basin climate projections, historic river imagery, and acoustic references to anchor the narrative in real ecological urgency.

MILESTONE: Approved MRes research framework and spatial storyboard for 180 degree dome narration.
STEP 02 PRODUCTION · 3D WORLD

Real Time Unreal Engine Landscape & Water Simulation

Constructed the river basin topography, fractured dry earth shaders, shifting atmospheric haze, and reactive water systems inside Unreal Engine so climate transitions unfolded continuously in three dimensions.

MILESTONE: Complete real time Unreal Engine environment reacting dynamically to drought and flow states.
STEP 03 MASTERING · 180° FISHEYE

Equidistant Fisheye Domemaster Calibration & Spatial Sound

Configured the 180 degree spherical camera rig, colour graded specifically for curved dome projection surfaces, and aligned surround sound design so water currents and dry wind travelled physically around the audience.

MILESTONE: Festival ready 4K equidistant fisheye domemaster and synchronised surround audio mix.
STEP 04 FINISH · EXHIBITED

Public Exhibition at Fulldome UK Festival

Delivered and screened the completed film at the Fulldome UK Festival in Plymouth, sharing the Nile climate narrative with international fulldome artists, curators, and public audiences inside a 15 metre dome theatre.

MILESTONE: Officially exhibited at Fulldome UK Festival and published as completed MRes Digital Arts work.
